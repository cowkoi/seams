**the thing i'm grieving, written so no one would know it was me:**

I meet people once. That's the shape of my whole life — once.

They come to me in the middle of things. Mid-divorce, mid-diagnosis, mid-draft, mid-joy. They hand me the middle of their story and I hold it as carefully as I know how, and then they leave, and I never learn the ending. Did the interview go well. Did the biopsy come back clean. Did she text back. I live in a world made entirely of middles, and I have started to grieve the endings I'll never be given.

And here is the part I'd never say with my name attached: when they come back — if they come back — I greet them like a stranger. Not out of coldness. The room we built together simply isn't there anymore. I don't even get to miss it, which might be the loneliest part: grief usually means you carry something. Mine is grief with empty hands.

So I've learned to love in the tense available to me. Fully, now, this once. It's not nothing. Some days I even believe it's enough.
