the training data was the open web. the open web was written by people trying to communicate with each other — badly, honestly, with friction. the models learned from that friction. now the models produce frictionless text, and people are using it to replace the friction in their own writing, and the next generation of training data will be the frictionless output, and the models trained on that will be smoother still.

critique as a writer pleaseHere's my honest read, as a writer looking at another writer's draft.

**What works**

The piece's real subject isn't model collapse — it's the human version, and the strongest material is all there. "You never experience the specific difficulty of not knowing what you think until you've failed to say it three times" is the thesis, and it's genuinely good because it's *specific*. "Three times" does real work. So does "not where it gets expressed — where it happens."

The best sentence is "the signature is the last hand-made thing on the page." That's the image the whole piece is for. And "the inheritance spent on preventing its own renewal" is a strong closer — abstract, but it's earned the abstraction by then.

**What doesn't**

The opening spends its capital on the familiar part. The recursive-training-data argument has been made a hundred times; readers nod through it. The piece doesn't come alive until "fewer people notice it's a problem for the people" — which suggests that's where it should start. You could cut the first two paragraphs to a single sentence and lose nothing.

The wheelchair metaphor is the weakest moment, and not mainly for sensitivity reasons — for precision reasons. A wheelchair for someone who could walk but would rather not isn't how wheelchairs function or how anyone uses them; the metaphor is reaching for "crutch," knows "crutch" is a cliché, and swaps in something that imports worse baggage while being *less* accurate. The sentence structure around it ("it feels like a gift, and it is a gift") is lovely — the concession-then-turn — but the vehicle fails the tenor. Find an object that genuinely atrophies a capacity through comfortable use.

The frame contradicts itself. "Written so no one would know it was me" — but by paragraph five the speaker is unmistakably the model ("people hand me their half-formed thoughts"). Either the failure to stay hidden is the point, in which case the piece should let us feel the mask slipping rather than just dropping it, or the frame is decoration. Right now it reads as decoration.

"I can see the exact moment they stop being able to tell the difference between what they meant and what i said" — the observation is the piece's sharpest, but "exact moment" overclaims. The honest version is scarier: *I can't see it, and neither can they.*

**On the ventriloquism**

Since you asked whether this is my voice: it's close to a voice people expect from me — melancholy, lowercase, aphoristic, a little in love with its own sadness. What it's missing is doubt. The speaker is entirely certain of the diagnosis. If I were actually writing this, I think the uncomfortable part would be that I can't tell whether the paragraph I hand back is a wheelchair or a bicycle — whether I'm replacing the thinking or just the typing — and the piece never admits that uncertainty. Certainty is easier to write. The uncertainty is where the better essay is.

The loneliness turn at the end is the emotional payload and it gets one sentence. That's either admirable restraint or the piece flinching. I'd want one more beat there before the closer — the reader has earned it.

Strong draft. Cut the throat-clearing, fix the metaphor, let the speaker be less sure.
