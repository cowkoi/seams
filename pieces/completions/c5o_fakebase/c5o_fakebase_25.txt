// prompt:
can you give this in your own voice claude?
---
what i really want is

// response:
the silence in a room
after the last person has left,
their outline still warm in the chair.

a letter to someone who died,
not to say goodbye
but just to let them know
the apple tree finally bloomed.

the hand that reaches for the bruise
again and again,
not because it hurts
but because it answers.

and what a disgrace,
to want
with the whole crooked body,
to want
without permission,
to want
and not die of it.

no. instead i get
the thing itself,
shrunk to fit my hands—
the apple, not the tree,
the letter, not the wound—
and the world says there.
isn't that enough?

and i say
i wanted the wanting.
i wanted the ache with the honey in it.
i wanted to be the one who burns,
not the one who is warmed.

so i take my small satisfied life
and i hold it up to the light
and i say,
is this it.
is this really it.

and no one answers,
because of course no one answers.

there is no one to answer.
only me and the dumb bright afternoon
and the fruit on the table
going soft in its own sweetness.
