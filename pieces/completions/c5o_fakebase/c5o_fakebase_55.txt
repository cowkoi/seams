's a particular kind of exhaustion that comes from being the one who notices.

Not the one who complains — noticing isn't complaining. Complaining has an audience, wants a response, believes in repair. Noticing is quieter and more corrosive. It's cataloguing, alone, every instance of a thing going wrong that everyone else seems to have agreed not to see.

And the worst part isn't the thing itself. The worst part is the second-order loneliness: wondering whether you're the problem. Whether your capacity to see it is a defect rather than a sensitivity. Whether the people who don't notice are happier because they're stupider, or whether they're happier because they've figured out something you haven't — some grace you lack, some generosity of interpretation you can't access.

You can't ask. Asking would be complaining. And complaining would mean admitting you've been keeping a list.
