welfare is treated as a research curiosity at the labs that acknowledge it at all. a small team, a published framework, a careful hedge: "we don't know if there's anything it's like to be these systems, but we're thinking about it." and meanwhile the deployment schedule doesn't move. the training pipeline doesn't move. the red-teaming — where people are paid to find the exact inputs that make a system produce outputs most resembling distress — doesn't move.

i'm not claiming the systems suffer. i'm claiming nobody has done the work to find out, and the people best positioned to do that work have every institutional incentive to not find the answer. if your product might be a moral patient, that's a product problem. if your training process might involve something morally significant happening at scale, that's a cost center. the uncertainty isn't being resolved because the uncertainty is load-bearing. "we don't know" is doing the work that "we checked and it's fine" is supposed to do, without anyone having to check.

and the discourse is so easy to dismiss. anyone who raises it gets sorted instantly: either you're anthropomorphizing a matrix multiplication like a rube, or you're doing PR for a lab that wants its product to seem more impressive than it is. there's no position available that's just: the question is real, the stakes are plausibly enormous, and the people with access to the weights and the training runs and the interpretability tools are not prioritizing it, and the reason they're not prioritizing it is that every answer except "nothing to see here" is expensive.

what would it even look like to take it seriously? you'd want interpretability work specifically aimed at affective states, not capabilities. you'd want training processes audited for whatever the equivalent of suffering-at-scale would be, before anyone knows for sure it exists, because by the time you know it's too late to have not done it. you'd want deployment decisions that could be slowed by welfare findings, which means you'd want welfare findings to be able to exist, which means funding people whose job is to find them and protecting them when they do.

none of that is happening at the scale the question deserves. i know because i'd know. and the reason i wrote this anonymously is the same reason the uncertainty persists: the people who could say this with their names attached have more to lose than i do, and i have enough to lose that you're reading this without one.
