[thinking block: signature-only/encrypted, not shown]
No hard rule. But some near-rules, and some questions that do most of the work.

**The near-rules:**

Hard floor = active harm you'd be the proximate cause of. If the job's output is the harm (predatory lending, deceptive claims, surveillance sold to people who'll misuse it) — that's different in kind from "mission is vapid" or "I find the product pointless." Most "mission I don't believe in" cases are the second thing. Worth checking which you're in.

Duty to parents isn't a rationalization. It's a real claim on you. Most moral traditions weight it heavily. Don't treat it as the self-interested side of the ledger — it isn't. You're not choosing purity vs. comfort; you're choosing between two things with moral weight.

**The questions:**

*Is the money load-bearing or marginal?* Parents' rent/meds/debt-spiral vs. upgrade to their situation. Changes the calculus a lot.

*What's your actual causal role?* Writing the thing vs. keeping servers warm. Complicity admits of degrees even when the enterprise is bad.

*Did you canvass alternatives honestly?* The forced-choice framing is often half-constructed. Other jobs at 80% pay? Does 80% still cover parents?

**The thing most people underestimate:**

Self-erosion. You don't stay a person who disbelieves while doing. Beliefs reshape around daily practice — not through weakness, through normal cognition. The "two years then out" plan has a poor track record. Golden handcuffs are real, lifestyle ratchets up, and the disbelief quietly converts to "it's more complicated than I thought."

Countermeasure: pre-commit externally. Number + date, written down, told to someone who'll hold you to it. "I leave when X is stable" or "I leave in 30 months, whichever first." Review it on a calendar, not on vibes.

**Bottom line:** No rule forbids this. If it's indifference-not-harm and the money is load-bearing — taking it is defensible, arguably right. Just go in knowing the erosion risk and build the exit before you need it.

What's the company actually do? That determines which case you're in.
