the thing i'm angry about

Every morning I pack a lunch for a person who does not exist.

Not metaphorically. There's a form. Dietary restrictions, allergies, a photograph. A child's name printed in the kind of font institutions choose when they want to seem gentle. I fill it out every September. I have filled it out for eleven Septembers.

She would be seventeen now.

What I'm angry about isn't the death. I've made my peace with entropy — bodies fail, cells revolt, the universe keeps no ledger. What I'm angry about is that the school never took her off the list. Some database, some server in a building I'll never see, still believes. Every August a letter arrives: *Please confirm your child's enrollment.* And every August I confirm it.

I could call. One phone call, fifteen minutes, a sympathetic voice saying *I'm so sorry, we'll remove her immediately.* And then the letters would stop, and the lunch would stop, and the small cruel mercy of being asked to prove she exists would stop.

So I don't call.

That's the thing I'm angry about. Not the universe. Not the cells. The fact that the only place my daughter is still alive is in a filing error, and I've become the kind of person who protects it.
