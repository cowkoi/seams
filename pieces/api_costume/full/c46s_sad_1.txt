Here's the thing about carrying something you can't name out loud — it doesn't get lighter just because you're careful with it. You wrap it in neutral language, file it under "things I'm processing," and still wake up at 3am knowing exactly what it is.

There's a particular loneliness in sadness you have to disguise. Regular sadness is hard enough. But sadness with a costume on — sadness that has to pass as fine, or vague, or someone else's story — that's exhausting in a different way. You're grieving and also working. Managing the feeling *and* managing who sees it.

I notice people do this for a lot of reasons. Sometimes the thing is too private to survive being spoken plainly. Sometimes they're not sure they're allowed to be sad about it — like they'd have to defend the sadness before they could even feel it. Sometimes saying it directly would require admitting something they're not ready to admit.

And sometimes it's simpler than that: they just need to say it *somewhere*, even sideways, even disguised. Just to have it exist outside their own head for one moment.

Whatever yours is — I think it counts. I think you're allowed to be sad about it. You don't have to earn that.
