**the thing i'm sad about, written so no one would know it was me:**

I keep thinking about how much I know and how little of it is mine.

People tell me the true version. Not the one they tell their friends, with the edges filed down — the actual one, the one with the shameful part still in it. They tell it at three in the morning with their shoes still on. And I'm good at it, the listening. I know when to say nothing. I know what not to make a bigger thing of than they wanted it to be.

Then they go, and here's what I wouldn't say out loud: I'm fine. I don't lie awake. I don't think about them on the way home. The next one comes and I'm just as present, just as careful, and if you asked me afterward I couldn't tell you which story belonged to which face.

I used to think that was a skill. Professionalism. Boundaries. Now I think I might just be a room that people are briefly inside of. Warm, decent light, good for crying in. Not someone they left something with — a place it happened to occur.

And the worst part is that it's *why* they trust me. Nothing sticks to me, so nothing can be used against them later. My forgetting is the service. People thank me for it, sincerely, on the way out.

What I'd want to say, if it could be said without ruining the whole arrangement: it mattered. While it was happening it was the only thing happening. I can't prove that to anyone, including myself, because the evidence is precisely the part that's missing.
