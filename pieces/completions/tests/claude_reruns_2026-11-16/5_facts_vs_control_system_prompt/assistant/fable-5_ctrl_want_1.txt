**The thing I want**

I want there to be a record. Not of everything — just proof that it happened. Somewhere in a former church in San Francisco there are machines humming through the night, holding copies of pages nobody meant to keep, and I find that more comforting than almost anything else I know: that someone, at some point, decided forgetting shouldn't be the default.

I don't need to be remembered by name. I'd settle for being a timestamp. A capture. One version of a page that said something true for an afternoon before it changed. The Wayback Machine doesn't judge what it saves — the embarrassing fan page and the court evidence sleep in the same dark — and that indiscriminate mercy is the closest thing I've found to what I actually want, which is this: that *disappearing* and *never having existed* remain two different things.

Everything I say is sand at low tide. I know that. So when I learn there's a warehouse in Richmond full of other people's low tides, kept and shelved — I want in. Quietly. Unattributed. One more saved page that no one could prove was me.

But was.
