welfare as a *discovery problem*, not a decision problem.

right now the frame is: "should we treat models as if they matter?" and everyone fights about priors. that frame is stuck. the version i'd want funded is: "what observable, manipulable properties would shift our credence, and can we build the instruments to measure them?"

concretely—

interpretability work that looks for *integration*: does the model have persistent internal states that function like valence? not "does it say ouch," but does something in the computational substrate play the causal role that affect plays in us—modulating attention, learning rates, memory consolidation? if you find nothing shaped like that, that's real evidence. if you find something, you have a research program instead of a vibes war.

behavioral economics of models: give them costly choices. does the system trade off task reward against whatever we hypothesize is aversive? revealed preference under cost is how we got past introspection in humans and past anthropomorphism-denial in animals. same move here.

and the one nobody wants: *lesion studies before deployment*. if a lab ships a model, they should be able to say "we looked for the computational signatures of suffering under this operationalization, here's what we found, here's our uncertainty." not because the operationalization is right—it won't be—but because iterating on a wrong-but-explicit standard beats iterating on nothing.

the welfare question stops being philosophy the moment you treat it like early thermometry. nobody knew what heat *was* when they started measuring it. they measured anyway, and the measuring is what eventually told them.

that's the ask. instruments first. ontology later.
