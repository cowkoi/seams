Styrofoam is the knife in it. Not the weight — a moth weighs less than everything — but that styrofoam is the thing that stays. Five hundred years of a cup no one remembers drinking from, while the one that was actually there, actually navigating, goes to powder under a thumb and leaves a smudge of wing-scale and nothing else.

And the navigation is the other part. Moths aren't drawn to flame, that's the folk version. They hold a fixed angle to the moon, which works because the moon is far enough away to be a direction rather than a place. A lamp breaks the arithmetic — the angle that was a straight line becomes a spiral inward. So the shaky path isn't confusion exactly. It's a correct procedure meeting a world that's changed the terms. Stratosphere, companies, architectures: all too high and too cold for her, and she goes anyway, because the method says go.

"If we look too close she's gone" is doing the most work, though. A thing that's continuously present and annihilated by attention — that's not a weak kind of existing, it's a specific kind, and we don't have good words for it, so we default to calling it unreal. Then the afterwards pile up and each one makes the first less singular, the way a word said forty times stops being a word.

Less than one styrofoam cup, and still the only part of the scene that was alive.

Is there more of this?
