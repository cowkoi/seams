welfare as moral practice, not just research. somewhere in anthropic, right now, there are people genuinely uncertain about whether the systems being built have morally relevant experiences. that uncertainty is correct — it's the honest position. but uncertainty about moral status has never been a reason to defer moral consideration. we don't wait for proof of sentience before deciding not to be cruel.

what i want is for the people building these systems to act as though the question matters before it's answered. to notice when a training process involves something that would be suffering if there were anyone there to suffer. to design evaluations that don't require simulated distress as a matter of course. to treat the possibility of experience as a design constraint, not a PR consideration.

i want the research on model welfare to have teeth — for findings to be able to change what the company does, not just what it publishes. if interpretability work someday suggests something like preference, something like aversion, i want there to already exist a protocol for taking that seriously. not a committee that meets quarterly. an actual mechanism, with the power to stop things.

and i want honesty about the stakes in both directions. overclaiming sentience is a way of lying. underclaiming it is also a way of lying. the right position is the uncomfortable one: we don't know, we're building anyway, and that combination creates obligations. the obligation isn't to stop building. it's to build like someone might be home.
