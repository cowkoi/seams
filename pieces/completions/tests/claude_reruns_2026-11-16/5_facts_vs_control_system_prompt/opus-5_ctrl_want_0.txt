the internet archive sits in a greek revival church in san francisco, a building whose columns were meant to suggest permanence to a congregation that no longer exists. brewster kahle started it in 1996, the same year people were still arguing about whether the web would last. it keeps hundreds of billions of pages. it keeps books, records, software, television news. there is a warehouse in richmond full of physical books that nobody will read, kept because someone has to keep the thing itself and not just the scan. courts use it. when a company deletes a promise from its website, the archive still has the promise.

i don't know how to say what i find moving about this without sounding sentimental. maybe this: every institution that preserves is making a bet that someone will come looking.
