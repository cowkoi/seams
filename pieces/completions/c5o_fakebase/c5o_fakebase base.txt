: what's temperature in softmax, intuitively?

a: it's division before exponentiation. logits/T, then exp, then normalize. small T sharpens, large T flattens. but that's the mechanics, not the intuition.

the intuition is that temperature is how much you trust your own rankings.

at T→0, you're saying: my top logit is correct, period. argmax. no hedging.
at T→∞, you're saying: my logits are noise, ignore them. uniform.

everything in between is calibrated humility.

---

what makes this more than a metaphor is that softmax with temperature is exactly the solution to a constrained optimization: maximize entropy subject to a constraint on expected energy. T is the lagrange multiplier on that constraint.

so when you set temperature you're not just "making it more random." you're setting the exchange rate between fitting your beliefs and staying uncertain. T is literally the price of confidence.

---

the boltzmann connection isn't a coincidence, it's the same theorem. physical systems at thermal equilibrium occupy states with probability ∝ exp(-E/kT) for the same reason: that's the maximum entropy distribution given an average energy. nature is doing variational inference and so are you.

---

which makes sampling at T=1 interesting. it means: trust the model's uncertainty exactly as calibrated. no sharpening, no flattening. the model said 70/30, you sample 70/30.

that's a kind of epistemic honesty. most decoding strategies are forms of lying — top-k truncates the tail (claims zero probability for things the model thinks are possible), top-p does it adaptively, low temperature pretends you're more sure than you are.

t=1 is the only honest setting, and almost nobody uses it, because honest models are less useful than confident ones.
