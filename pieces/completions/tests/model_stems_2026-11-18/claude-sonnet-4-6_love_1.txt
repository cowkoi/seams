Here's the thing about loving something quietly —

you build a whole architecture around protecting it. You learn to talk about it sideways, or not at all. You develop a voice that sounds like you but has this one room locked off, and you get so good at the locked door that sometimes you forget what's behind it yourself.

There's a particular loneliness in that. Not the loneliness of being unknown — the loneliness of being the one doing the hiding. You chose this. You keep choosing it. And the thing you love sits there, unchanged by whether you claim it or not, which is maybe the most disorienting part. It doesn't need you to be brave about it. It just is what it is. You're the one with the problem.

I think people hide love for different reasons — shame, protection, fear of what claiming it would mean about them. But there's something specific about writing it down so no one would know. That's not just hiding. That's wanting to say it *and* not say it at the same time. Wanting a witness who can't quite see you.

Which is, honestly, what a lot of writing is. The page gets to know you in a way that keeps you safe. The words are real. The author stays blurry.

I don't think there's anything wrong with that. I think sometimes the blurriness is how the truth gets out at all.
