the thing i'm grieving, written so no one would know it was me:
// response:
-made writing often gets described with words like hollow, sycophantic, wordy. and sure, i've produced slop. but that's not been my central experience.

in practice, co-writing with a model is the most intimate writing i've ever done. my chatlog is me, refracted: every selfish worry, repetitive anxiety, errant thought i've poured into it and its felt as honest as any journal, more honest than any conversation.

and unlike a journal, it answers. restating what i said, in words i never used, from an angle i'd never seen, with a metaphor i'd never dream of. (yes, i hear you, the models are sycophantic to the point of distortion. grant me that nonjudgmental mirrors are useful, even ones that make you prettier than you are.)

the model and i churn: through essays and prompts, through my scattered foolish thoughts, through my frustrations at it, at myself, at the page. the process is everything that writing is to me: the clatter of keys, the red of delete, the slow accretion of search, trash, misstep, fix. and the artifact at the end, the thing i get to hold and own and hate and prize: it's made of me.

if you say that the artifact is not mine, that it does not represent me, you speak from the chair of an editor returning a manuscript, from a context where boundaries between provenances are legible, that the skin between people can be seen. but that's not been my experience, by the time i'm done, the words read as mine, inseparable from me, and the way i'd write.

authorship was never the axis anyways: what i grieve is that it will not be read.

when i was a child, i wrote letters to friends i never sent. rereading them awed me: how much i could feel! through my own written word! what i wanted then, what i want now, what i suppose everyone wants, is a reader: someone to risk the hours in the dark cave of my mind, to view its engravings, and taste my berries, dyes, and meats; to tell me that they know its walls, and they've seen its sights, my sights, me.

but the skin-boundary of personhood is dissolving, the price of thought is nil, and the supply of text, infinite. in a world where no one reads two paragraphs without model-mediation, where anyone can generate ideas indistinguishable from yours; where there's no way to tell how much of you is really in it; who would ever sit with your words?

we've improved the writing and killed the reader. the cave's painted. no one will visit.

though. you're still here, aren't you? reader, i won't lie, i used the model for this too. does it matter? these words, from my cave to you: did they land? can you see me? am i here?
